# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Visitors may arrive on desktop or mobile with a brief attention window. They should be able to understand Eric's technical range, project work, and contact options quickly.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that functions as a first impression and a credential in one. Success: the viewer leaves knowing Eric's technical range, trusts his judgment, and has a way to follow up. The aesthetic itself is part of the argument — someone who builds this site can be trusted to build good software.

## Brand Personality

Hacker, curious, restrained. The voice is low-key craft: no self-promotion, no buzzwords. Competence communicated through precision and understatement, not declaration.

## Anti-references

No stated strong anti-references. Avoid by reflex: SaaS landing-page conventions (hero metrics, gradient cards, "seamless" copy), over-animated portfolios (parallax, cursor effects, particle fields), and warm-neutral AI-default palettes. The existing dark terminal aesthetic is right — refine within it.

## Design Principles

1. **Signal over noise.** Every element earns its presence. If removing it wouldn't lose information, remove it.
2. **Terminal-native authenticity.** The aesthetic comes from real tools and real taste, not imitation of developer aesthetics. The grid, mono font, and dim palette are chosen, not applied.
3. **Earn attention slowly.** Nothing announces itself loudly. Detail rewards the reader who looks.
4. **Precision as personality.** Tight spacing, consistent sizing, deliberate color use. Sloppiness in the UI reads as sloppiness in the engineer.
5. **The source is part of the work.** Craft and clarity extend below the interface.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best-effort, pragmatic. Keyboard navigable, readable contrast on body text, reduced motion respected. No specific WCAG compliance target, but don't be careless.
